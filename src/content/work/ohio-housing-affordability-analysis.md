## Overview

Statewide housing averages can make affordability look relatively stable while hiding substantial differences between individual counties. This project uses U.S. Census American Community Survey (ACS) data from 2019–2023 to examine how rent, income, home values, and rent burden vary across Ohio.

The analysis moves from statewide trends to county-level comparisons, then takes a closer look at Sandusky County and how its affordability profile compares with neighboring counties and statewide benchmarks. The project was motivated by volunteer experience related to housing affordability in Sandusky County.


## Questions Explored

- How have rent, income, and rent burden changed across Ohio counties from 2019 to 2023?
- How much variation is hidden by statewide affordability averages?
- Where do higher rent burdens appear across the state?
- How does Sandusky County compare with neighboring counties and statewide benchmarks?
- Have rising rents translated into greater rent burden, or has income growth offset some of the increase?


## Statewide Patterns

Across Ohio counties, average median gross rent increased from the mid-$700s in 2019 to nearly $900 by 2023. Over the same period, the average rent-to-income ratio declined modestly from approximately 16% to 15%, suggesting that income growth offset some of the increase in rents at the statewide level.

That statewide picture, however, hides substantial county-level variation. Rent-to-income ratios ranged from roughly 12% to 23%, with some counties maintaining considerably higher burdens than the statewide average.

The geographic analysis also showed that higher-burden counties were scattered across Ohio rather than concentrated in one part of the state, reinforcing the value of examining affordability at the county level rather than relying on statewide averages alone.

## Sandusky County in Context

Sandusky County remained near the middle of Ohio counties throughout the 2019–2023 period rather than consistently appearing at either end of the affordability distribution.

Its rent-to-income ratio declined through 2022 before increasing slightly in 2023. When compared with neighboring counties, Sandusky County generally occupied a middle position, with rent and income levels following the broader regional pattern.

The statewide benchmark comparison tells a similar story. Sandusky County's rent burden remained below the Ohio average and below the 75th percentile of counties across the period. Its statewide ranking remained approximately 50–58, keeping it within the middle 50% of Ohio counties from 2019 through 2023.


## Key Findings

- Rising rents did not translate into an equivalent increase in statewide rent burden because household income also increased over the period.
- Statewide averages concealed meaningful differences between counties, with rent-to-income ratios ranging from roughly 12% to 23%.
- Higher rent burden was geographically dispersed rather than confined to one region of Ohio.
- Sandusky County remained relatively stable in its statewide position and consistently fell within the middle 50% of counties.
- Sandusky County's rent burden remained below the statewide average and higher-burden benchmarks throughout the period.

## Analytical Perspective

The analysis shows why housing affordability is challenging to understand from a single statewide number. Temporal trends reveal whether conditions are changing, county comparisons expose local variation, maps show where those differences occur, and benchmarks provide context for interpreting an individual county.

For Sandusky County, these views point in the same direction: affordability pressure exists, but its rent burden during this period was not unusually high relative to Ohio or its surrounding counties. Its pattern was closer to broader state and regional conditions than to an exceptional local affordability problem.
